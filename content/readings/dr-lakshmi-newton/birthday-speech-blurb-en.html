On the sacred day of Kartika Purnima, as Quantum Life University celebrated the birth anniversary of Brahmarshi Chakravarti Dr. Newton Kondaveeti, Dr. Lakshmi Newton rose to speak. What followed was not a formal address. It was a living transmission — part remembrance, part story, part call to the soul.

She began with simple, heartfelt reverence. Bowing to the lotus feet of her parents, remembering the ever-present chair kept for Patriji, and holding space for the lineage of masters who have walked beside their journey, she turned the gathering’s attention to the man of the day. Dr. Newton, she said, is a multi-dimensional master — not merely because of what he teaches, but because of how he lives.

With the tenderness of one who has walked the path closely, she painted a vivid portrait. In the midst of the assembly he smiles like the playful Balakrishna. When it is time to pour love, he becomes the Krishna of Vrindavan. And when the moment demands uncompromising truth, he stands like the Krishna of Kurukshetra, offering knowledge that cuts through every excuse. She recalled the day he simply asked her,

“Will you come with me?”
and she answered in a single breath, without a second thought. In that instant she dropped the two great obstacles of the spiritual path — mamakara (the sense of “mine”) and ahankara (the sense of “I”).

To make the teaching unforgettable, she shared the classic story of Mulla Nasruddin and the borrowed coat. With gentle humour she showed how even a small piece of clothing can become the ground for endless identification and suffering. The story landed as a mirror. If we cannot release a coat, how will we release a lifetime of attachments when the call of the higher life comes?

She then opened the pages of their early years — thirty years ago, when two seekers set out with almost nothing. There were nights with no place to sleep, days when food came only once in weeks, and entire stretches when the only meal was a fruit found along the way. As pure vegetarians travelling through places where vegetarian food was scarce, they learned to live with complete uncertainty. Yet the joy of the mission never dimmed. The story was not told for sympathy. It was told as evidence that when the heart is fixed on the work of spreading meditation and self-knowledge, the outer circumstances lose their power to decide the journey.

Then came the COVID years — the moment the entire world stopped. Workshops vanished, programmes disappeared, and hundreds of people who depended on the work looked to them for support. In that silence, the depth of their commitment was tested once more. What emerged was not contraction, but a stronger resolve: the knowledge of the Self, the Brahma Jnana that India has always carried, must once again become the offering of this land to the world.

She closed with a clear, luminous sankalpa. Let every seeker, every student, every heart that has tasted this wisdom help make Bharat a place where the world comes for Atma Jnana and Brahma Jnana. Let Quantum Life University become the living centre of that renaissance. With the traditional cry of

“Bharat Mata ki Jai”
and a simple, overflowing
“Thank you,”
she returned the stage to the silence from which the entire message had arisen.

It was a birthday speech. It was also a map of the path — playful and profound, personal and universal, grounded in real hardship and lit by unwavering vision. Those who heard it left not only with affection for the day, but with a quiet fire to live the teaching.
