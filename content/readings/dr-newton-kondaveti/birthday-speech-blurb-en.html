On the luminous day of Kartika Purnima, as Quantum Life University gathered to celebrate the birth of Brahmarshi Chakravarti Dr. Newton Kondaveeti, the man himself rose to speak. What unfolded was not a list of achievements, but a quiet, profound offering of thanks — a birthday message that became a teaching on lineage, grace, and the mystery of the Self.

He began at the very beginning.

Today is my birthday. I was born as a small child,
he said, and immediately turned his heart toward the two people who gave him this body: his mother, Smt. Kondaveeti Radhamma, and his father, Sri Kondaveeti Kodanda Ramayya. With deep gratitude he acknowledged them as the ones he chose as parents, and who chose him as their son. From them, he said, he learned the foundational truth that mother, father, guru and God are the four pillars of life.

From there his gaze moved to the one who walks beside him. He spoke of Dr. Lakshmi Newton not merely as his life partner, but as his own spiritual master — a being who, in his eyes, stands several steps higher on the path. When she holds the microphone, he said, it is as if Kali Mata, Durga Mata, Saraswati Mata and Lakshmi Mata themselves are speaking. Every sentence that leaves her lips, he confessed, lands with the clarity of a pot broken open, settling forever in the hearts of those who hear.

Then came the deepest bow of all — to his guru, Brahmasri Pitamaha Patriji. With countless salutations he remembered the promise Patriji made years ago: that on every birthday he would be present, no matter where in the world he might be. Since 2007 that promise has been kept. A chair is always reserved. The guru’s presence, Dr. Newton said, needs no words. A single glance is enough. In that glance lives the ocean of compassion that has guided his entire journey.

In the most intimate moment of the speech, he shared a recent inner experience that has been unfolding for three months: the direct recognition of the Paramatma Tattva — the Supreme Self. For years the question had lived within him:

Who is Paramatma? I must know.
Then one day the experience arrived. Standing as the individual self (jivatma) within this body that has now completed 57 years and entered its 58th, he offered thanks even to that Supreme Reality for allowing this small self to appear here.

He closed by turning outward — to the team he affectionately called his “vanara sena,” the seva mitras who had made the entire celebration possible, and to the many well-wishers whose love continues to sustain the work. Ordinary birthdays bring gifts, he noted. But when Brahma Jnanis are present, the gifts are of a different order: knowledge, a university, countless companions on the path, and the living opportunity to serve. These, he said, are the true presents.

It was a birthday speech unlike any other. Simple, transparent, and luminous with gratitude, it reminded everyone present that the real celebration of a life is not what one has achieved, but what one has been given — and what one continues to offer in return.
